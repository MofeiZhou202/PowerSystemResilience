The numerical study treats SPPT as a verification layer rather than a new
power-flow algorithm. It asks four questions: \textbf{RQ1}, can heterogeneous
public distribution models be converted into disclosed hybrid AC/DC variants
without hiding conversion scope; \textbf{RQ2}, which structural and
converter-role faults are missed when solver convergence is used as the
admission criterion; \textbf{RQ3}, can accepted quantities and rejected edits
be traced to rich-model devices and fields; and \textbf{RQ4}, what remains
outside the guarantee when typed AI actions or external files supply plausible
but incorrect data?

\subsection{Public distribution cases and reproducible protocol}

\paragraph{Distribution-feeder ladder (RQ1).}
The primary corpus consists of seven public GridLAB-D r5643 taxonomy feeders
\cite{gridlabd}, not transmission test systems. The existing GridLAB-D interface
loads each GLM file in permissive mode and returns its warnings and skipped
objects. The resulting positive-sequence models range from 32 to 6986 AC buses
and from 21 to 5196 AC branches (\Cref{fig:public-feeders}). All seven imports
have zero skipped objects; the 4--61 warnings are retained as conversion-scope
evidence rather than silently discarded. In particular, this importer supports
the balanced steady-state subset used here and does not claim full preservation
of unbalanced phase-domain controls, protection, or the complete GridLAB-D
object library.

Each imported feeder receives the same fully specified, deterministic
two-terminal DC overlay. A PQ-controlled VSC is connected to the AC bus carrying
the largest imported active load and a VDC-Q converter to the imported source
bus. The converters connect two DC buses through one line with
$r_{\rm dc}=0.02$~p.u.; the voltage-forming converter regulates
$V_{\rm dc}=1.02$~p.u.; both converters use $\eta=0.99$. The PQ transfer is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\rm PQ}=\operatorname{clamp}
 \left(0.02P_{\rm load,total},\,0.001S_{\rm base},\,0.05S_{\rm base}\right).
 \label{eq:public-overlay}
\end{equation}
Thus the hybridization is reproducible from public data and the stated rule;
it is not calibrated to an undisclosed utility network. The machine-readable
manifest records the source file, imported counts, warnings/skips, AC and DC
terminals, control modes, setpoints, efficiency, and DC branch for every case.

\paragraph{Fixed campaign.}
The prespecified generator uses MT19937-64 with seed 20260818 and 25 repetitions
per case and edit class. It produces
$7\times9\times25=1575$ rows: 175 valid component-order permutations, 1050
structural faults, and 350 plausible intention errors. The six structural
classes remove a VSC AC terminal, remove a VSC DC terminal, remove the AC angle
reference, remove physical DC-voltage support, set converter efficiency above
unity, or duplicate an AC-bus identity. The two intention-error classes multiply
one imported load by a factor in $[0.70,1.30]$ or apply a nonzero signed
perturbation to the in-service PQ-VSC active-power setpoint. No failed,
nonconverged, or inconvenient sample is removed.

Five admission chains are recorded: boundary validation, solver-only,
validation plus solver, the full SPPT guard, and SPPT plus an independently
implemented primitive-equation residual. A solver-only rejection means
nonconvergence, a nonfinite residual, or a residual greater than $10^{-6}$.
Detection means refusing an injected structural fault; localization additionally
requires the diagnostic to identify its component and field, or its affected
AC/DC island reference. The 95\% intervals use the Wilson score interval. To
bound computational cost without selecting outcomes, structural solver-only
trials are fixed to the 32-, 78-, and 330-bus cases, whereas physical-impact
solves are fixed to the 32-, 78-, 330-, and 823-bus cases. Every case remains in
validation, projection, attribution, and full-guard tests.

\subsection{Structural safety benefit and solver blind spots}

\paragraph{Detection and well-posedness (RQ2).}
The full SPPT chain detects and localizes all 1050 structural faults. Pooling the
six classes gives 1.000 detection with a 95\% Wilson interval
$[0.9964,1.000]$. It rejects none of the 175 valid permutations, corresponding
to a false-reject estimate of zero and a 95\% upper bound of 0.0215. These
controls are important: perfect fault detection alone could otherwise be
obtained by rejecting every model.

\Cref{fig:structural-detection} separates the guard layers. Boundary
validation detects terminal, efficiency, identity, and AC-reference faults, but
misses all 175 missing-DC-support cases. Converter-role typing closes precisely
this gap. Solver-only admission is weaker: among the three in-scope feeders it
misses all dangling VSC terminals, all missing DC support, and all invalid
efficiencies; it rejects the missing AC reference and only 3 of 75 duplicate
identities. Hence numerical convergence establishes a root of the assembled
equations, not completeness or physical meaning of the authored model.

  full-SPPT results use 175 samples per class across all seven public feeder

\paragraph{Layer ablation.}
The comparison is also a functional ablation. Removing role typing from the
full chain recreates the 0/175 missing-DC-support result of validation. Removing
validation and retaining only the nonlinear solver recreates four complete
blind spots plus the 4\% duplicate-identity rejection rate. Adding an
independent residual does not change structural counts because those samples
are rejected before solution; its distinct purpose is to check an admitted
state against equations independent of the primary analysis path.

\subsection{Provenance, attribution, and the intent-error boundary}

\paragraph{Device-level auditability (RQ3).}
Every valid permutation passes the guard's requested-observable attribution
gate for AC-bus voltage, DC-bus voltage, and converter transfer. The projection
therefore remains invariant to container order while results are addressed by
stable rich-model identity. For rejected edits, all 1050 diagnostics return to
the injected device/field or affected island reference. Independent round-trip
checks cover intensive bus-voltage broadcast and extensive allocation after bus
merging; unsupported observables fail the attribution gate rather than receiving
fabricated values. Campaign localization audits the input fault, whereas
round-trip evaluation audits solver-to-device reconstruction.

\paragraph{Structurally admissible but wrong values (RQ4).}
Neither SPPT nor an outlier detector can infer operator intent from a single
self-consistent model. All 350 plausible load and VSC-setpoint edits preserve
references, limits, identities, and equation consistency, so all structural
chains admit them. Their physical consequences are nevertheless nonzero
(\Cref{fig:intent-impact}). On the four feeder variants with impact solves, a
load edit gives median/95th-percentile/maximum AC-voltage deviations of
$3.089\times10^{-4}$/$1.652\times10^{-3}$/$4.239\times10^{-3}$~p.u. and branch
active-power deviations of 0.00524/0.427/0.526~MW. A VSC-setpoint edit gives
AC-voltage deviations of $1.821\times10^{-4}$/$1.828\times10^{-3}$/$1.916\times10^{-3}$~p.u.,
DC-voltage deviations of $3.706\times10^{-5}$/$5.844\times10^{-5}$/$6.322\times10^{-5}$~p.u.,
branch-flow deviations of 0.0192/0.0300/0.0320~MW, and converter-transfer
deviations of 0.0189/0.0298/0.0322~MW. Here the empirical 95th percentile is the
nearest observed order statistic. The zero DC/VSC response to load edits follows
from the fixed PQ transfer in \Cref{eq:public-overlay}; it is not missing data.

This experiment marks the contract boundary rather than a failure of the
contract. Parameter provenance, telemetry consistency, state estimation,
operator confirmation, or anomaly detection must provide the external evidence
needed to decide whether a numerically plausible value was intended. SPPT then
checks whether the resulting edited model is structurally valid, well posed,
solvable, and attributable.

\subsection{Typed-agent experiment}

The agent experiment evaluates the transactional workflow in
\cref{fig:agent-workflow}, rather than treating a prompt response as a power-flow
result. Six typed actions are applied independently to every public hybrid
feeder: a 10\% load increase, an identity action, two nonexistent VSC terminal
assignments, removal of DC-voltage support, and an above-unity converter
efficiency. Each candidate action is followed by an identity recovery action,
which tests whether a rejected candidate has altered the admitted state.

All 14 admissible system--action combinations are accepted and committed, while
all 28 inadmissible combinations are rejected without commit
(\cref{fig:agent-campaign}). Every recovery action is subsequently accepted.
End-to-end power flow is prespecified for the 32-, 78-, 330-, and 823-bus
variants: all eight admissible primary actions and all 24 recovery actions in
that scope converge and return attributed bus voltages. The three larger
feeders remain in guard, commit, and recovery evaluation but are not repeatedly
solved. An unexecuted large-case solve is therefore not counted as either
convergence or failure.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/sppt_agent_public_campaign.pdf}
  \caption{Typed-agent evaluation on seven public hybrid distribution feeders.
  (a) Every admissible action is committed and every inadmissible action is
  rejected before commit. (b) Rejected actions do not invoke analysis, and the

This campaign is evidence for typed action safety, state isolation, and
solver-to-device attribution. It is not a benchmark of unrestricted language
understanding. An optional five-prompt local LLM run is retained only as a
supplementary interface-feasibility demonstration; its sample size is not used
as statistical evidence of LLM reliability.

\subsection{Three-phase and external-engine scope checks}

The primary campaign uses the imported balanced subset because the public GLM
interface does not preserve full phase-domain control semantics. A staged
three-phase boundary study therefore exercises three disclosed distribution
variants: a balanced radial feeder, an unbalanced lateral feeder, and an
unbalanced meshed feeder with DER, each coupled to a two-bus DC charger. All
three base points converge. Across load multipliers
$\{0.5,1.0,1.25,1.5,1.75,2.0\}$, the balanced, lateral, and meshed variants
converge at 6/6, 4/6, and 5/6 DC-boundary points, respectively. The failed high
stress points are retained and reported; this staged study demonstrates a
phase-aware workflow boundary, not equivalence to the positive-sequence
campaign.

External-engine checks are similarly scope qualified. The GridLAB-D bridge
imports and solves the projected AC snapshots of all 24 public taxonomy files;
only seven legacy files also execute in the available modern GridLAB-D runtime,
so numerical comparison is restricted to their 3222 common buses. The OpenDSS
study executes the official IEEE-13 and six supplied public EPRI circuits, but
is used only for overlapping three-phase AC semantics, not for the added DC grid
or VSC controls. These checks expose conversion boundaries rather than declaring
one engine a universal hybrid AC/DC oracle.

\subsection{Reproducibility and validity limits}

The protocol records the public source feeder, deterministic overlay, action
type, target, magnitude, seed, verdict, localization, solver status, independent
residual, and physical impact in machine-readable form. An independent repeat
reproduces all 1575 non-timing records field-for-field; the four wall-clock
fields are deliberately excluded. Figures are generated from these records
rather than manually transcribed. No execution-time comparison is reported
because the study evaluates correctness and admission behavior, not a new
power-flow algorithm.

Perfect detection in this finite injected population is not a universal
completeness theorem. The campaign samples independent typed edits, not
correlated cyber-physical attacks, telemetry drift, or prompt distributions.
The largest feeders exercise parsing, projection, attribution, and the guard,
but repeated physical-impact solves stop at 823 AC buses by prespecified budget.
Finally, GridLAB-D warnings and external-engine disagreements are scope evidence,
not values to suppress. Within these limits, the result is specific and
actionable: solver convergence is an unsafe admission proxy for converter-
dominated distribution models, whereas semantics-preserving projection,
converter-role typing, and total attribution provide a reproducible verification
layer around AI-assisted modeling.
