% ============================================================
\section{Three-Stage Fault-Recovery Reliability Analysis}
\label{sec:three_stage_reliability}
% ============================================================

This chapter is the code-synchronised contract for the native C++ three-stage
fault-recovery reliability evaluator in
\texttt{src/reliability/three\_stage\_reliability.cpp}.  The historical Julia
process bridge has been removed.  The current implementation reads the normal
\texttt{HybridPowerSystem} JSON schema, builds a native case, and solves staged
load-restoration subproblems with the embedded MIPSolvers stack.  Pure LP
subproblems use the native simplex route; true MILPs use HiGHS first and the
native B\&C engine only as fallback.

The implemented physical model is intentionally narrower than the full hybrid
research formulation.  AC restoration is a finite-source LinDistFlow MILP.  DC
buses, DC branches, VSC links, DC/DC converters, and SOP devices are handled by
a connectivity/capacity fallback after the AC MILP.  VSC/SOP dispatch is not
co-optimised, and the exported \texttt{psop} vectors are filled with zeroes.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Three Operational Stages}
\label{sec:3stage-background}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

For every simulated line contingency, the evaluator applies the same AC
restoration model with stage-specific network availability:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Stage \arabic*:}]
  \item \textbf{Fault isolation} ($0 \le t \le \tau_{\mathrm{SW}}$): the
        faulted edge is open and normally-open restoration candidates remain
        open.
  \item \textbf{Post-fault reconfiguration}
        ($\tau_{\mathrm{SW}} < t \le \tau_{\mathrm{TP}}$): the faulted edge
        remains open; normally-open AC tie branches and standalone AC switches
        may close subject to \texttt{max\_switch\_operations}.
  \item \textbf{Post-repair reconfiguration}
        ($\tau_{\mathrm{TP}} < t \le \tau_{\mathrm{RP}}$): the repaired edge is
        available again and normally-open candidates return to their base-state
        treatment.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1.0cm,y=1.0cm]
  \draw[->] (0,0) -- (11.5,0) node[right] {$t$};
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/0, 3/$\tau_{\mathrm{SW}}$,
                        7/$\tau_{\mathrm{TP}}$, 11/$\tau_{\mathrm{RP}}$}
    \draw (\x, 0.08) -- (\x,-0.08) node[below,font=\small] {\lab};
  \draw[fill=red!20,draw=red!60] (0,0.2) rectangle (3,0.8)
    node[midway,font=\small] {Stage 1};
  \draw[fill=orange!20,draw=orange!60] (3,0.2) rectangle (7,0.8)
    node[midway,font=\small] {Stage 2};
  \draw[fill=green!20,draw=green!60] (7,0.2) rectangle (11,0.8)
    node[midway,font=\small] {Stage 3};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

Reliability indices use Stage~1 and Stage~2 interruption windows.  Stage~3 is
still solved and reported for per-fault diagnostics, but it occurs after repair
and therefore does not add EENS in the current index equations.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Native Case Construction}
\label{sec:3stage-native-case}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\texttt{build\_native\_case()} constructs the model data used by all stages:

\begin{itemize}
  \item AC load points are formed once from explicit \texttt{ac.loads} and from
        direct \texttt{ACBus::pd\_mw}/\texttt{qd\_mvar}.  Bus-level demand is
        not added a second time after load-point aggregation.
  \item Source buses include in-service AC generators, external grids, storage,
        renewable/static generation, PV systems, and source-like projected
        records with positive active capacity.
  \item Candidate AC edges include in-service AC branches, out-of-service AC tie
        branches, and standalone AC switches.  Standalone normally-open switches
        are valid Stage~2 restoration candidates.
  \item Fault generation simulates in-service AC/DC branches.  Normally-open
        out-of-service tie edges are not treated as faulted energized branches.
\end{itemize}

The model does not create an infinite slack bus.  Every source has finite active
and reactive capacity bounds.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Implemented AC Restoration MILP}
\label{sec:3stage-implemented-milp}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

For a stage and contingency, let $\mathcal{B}$ be the AC bus set,
$\mathcal{L}$ the AC candidate-edge set, and
$\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{B}$ the finite-source bus set.  For bus $i$,
$P^d_i,Q^d_i$ are aggregated demands and
$\bar P^g_i,\bar Q^g_i$ are source capacities.  For edge
$\ell=(i,j)$, $\bar S_\ell$ is its thermal limit or documented fallback rating.

\paragraph{Decision variables.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Variable & Domain & Meaning \\
\hline
$p^{sh}_i$ & $[0,P^d_i]$ & active load shed at bus $i$ \\
$y_i$ & $\{0,1\}$ & bus $i$ energized/served; source buses are fixed on \\
$z_\ell$ & $\{0,1\}$ & edge $\ell$ used in the energized AC forest \\
$P_\ell,Q_\ell$ & continuous & active/reactive LinDistFlow edge flow \\
$v_i$ & $[\underline V_i^2,\overline V_i^2]$ & squared voltage magnitude \\
$f_\ell$ & continuous & signed radiality commodity flow \\
$p^g_i,q^g_i$ & bounded continuous & finite source dispatch \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The code offsets in \texttt{solve\_stage\_milp()} correspond directly to these
symbols:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Code offset & Mathematical symbol & Primary role \\
\hline
\texttt{off\_shed} & $p^{sh}_i$ & load-shed objective and balance \\
\texttt{off\_y} & $y_i$ & energized-bus coupling \\
\texttt{off\_z} & $z_\ell$ & branch status, switching, forest constraints \\
\texttt{off\_P}, \texttt{off\_Q} & $P_\ell,Q_\ell$ & AC LinDistFlow balance and limits \\
\texttt{off\_v} & $v_i$ & voltage envelope and drops \\
\texttt{off\_f} & $f_\ell$ & commodity-flow radiality \\
\texttt{off\_pg}, \texttt{off\_qg} & $p^g_i,q^g_i$ & finite source dispatch \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Objective and pickup coupling.}
\begin{align}
\min \quad & \sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}} p^{sh}_i ,
  \label{eq:cxx-3stage-obj}\\
P^d_i - p^{sh}_i &\le P^d_i y_i,
  && \forall i\in\mathcal{B}. \label{eq:cxx-energized-load}
\end{align}
If $y_i=0$, all active load at bus $i$ must be shed.  If $y_i=1$, partial
shedding is allowed when finite sources or branch capacities bind.

\paragraph{Finite-source active/reactive balance.}
For every AC bus, including source buses,
\begin{align}
\sum_{\ell=(j,i)} P_\ell - \sum_{\ell=(i,j)} P_\ell + p^g_i + p^{sh}_i
  &= P^d_i, && \forall i\in\mathcal{B}, \label{eq:cxx-p-balance}\\
\sum_{\ell=(j,i)} Q_\ell - \sum_{\ell=(i,j)} Q_\ell + q^g_i
  + \rho_i p^{sh}_i &= Q^d_i,
  && \forall i\in\mathcal{B}, \label{eq:cxx-q-balance}\\
0 \le p^g_i &\le \bar P^g_i, \qquad
0 \le q^g_i \le \bar Q^g_i,
  && \forall i\in\mathcal{B}. \label{eq:cxx-source-bounds}
\end{align}
Here $\rho_i=Q^d_i/P^d_i$ when $P^d_i>0$ and zero otherwise.  The current
implementation uses a fallback 0.9 power-factor reactive model when source JSON
does not provide richer reactive demand.

\paragraph{Branch physics and switching.}
For edge $\ell=(i,j)$,
\begin{align}
|v_j-v_i+2(r_\ell P_\ell+x_\ell Q_\ell)/S_{base}| &\le M_v(1-z_\ell),
  \label{eq:cxx-vdrop}\\
-\bar S_\ell z_\ell \le P_\ell &\le \bar S_\ell z_\ell, \qquad
-\bar S_\ell z_\ell \le Q_\ell \le \bar S_\ell z_\ell.
  \label{eq:cxx-branch-limits}
\end{align}
The faulted AC edge is fixed open before repair.  Stage~2 normally-open
candidates may close, limited by
$\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}^{NO}} z_\ell \le K^{sw}$ when
\texttt{max\_switch\_operations} is finite.

\paragraph{Strict radial energized forest.}
Let $C=|\mathcal{B}|-1$.  The radiality constraints are
\begin{align}
-Cz_\ell \le f_\ell &\le Cz_\ell, && \forall\ell\in\mathcal{L},
  \label{eq:cxx-f-bound}\\
z_{(i,j)} &\le y_i, \qquad z_{(i,j)} \le y_j,
  && \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}, \label{eq:cxx-z-y}\\
\sum_{\ell=(j,i)} f_\ell - \sum_{\ell=(i,j)} f_\ell &= y_i,
  && \forall i\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{S}, \label{eq:cxx-flow-load}\\
\sum_{\ell=(j,i)} f_\ell - \sum_{\ell=(i,j)} f_\ell &\le 0,
  && \forall i\in\mathcal{S}, \label{eq:cxx-flow-source}\\
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}} z_\ell &\le
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}} y_i - |\mathcal{S}|. \label{eq:cxx-forest-count}
\end{align}
These rows jointly enforce that energized non-source buses connect to a source
and that the energized topology contains no redundant cycles.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Hybrid Fallback and Validity Flags}
\label{sec:3stage-hybrid-scope}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The AC MILP above is the only optimisation model solved for restoration.
Hybrid components are represented as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item DC loads and DC sources enter a graph-based connectivity/capacity
        fallback; no DC voltage or branch-flow equation is enforced.
  \item VSC and DC/DC converters are graph edges for connectivity/capacity
        transfer only; converter loss and dispatch constraints are not
        co-optimised.
  \item SOP configurations are echoed in \texttt{sop\_config}, but
        \texttt{psop1}, \texttt{psop2}, and \texttt{psop3} are zero vectors.
\end{itemize}

Pure AC cases report \texttt{model\_scope="ac-lindistflow-milp"}.  Hybrid
cases report
\texttt{model\_scope="ac-lindistflow-milp+dc-connectivity-fallback"}.  The
whole-result validity flags are true for AC branch flow, voltage, radiality,
and restoration MILP only when the evaluated system is pure AC.  In hybrid
cases these flags are deliberately false because the DC/VSC/SOP portion is
fallback-only, even though the AC subproblem itself was solved.

\subsection{Reliability Index Calculation}
\label{sec:3stage-indices}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Let $p^{sh,(s)}_{d,l}$ be the active load shed in stage $s$ at load point $d$
when line $l$ fails.  With annual failure rate $\lambda_l$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{CIF}_d &= \sum_l \lambda_l\,
\mathbf{1}\!\left[p^{sh,(1)}_{d,l}>0\right], \label{eq:cif}\\
\mathrm{CID}_d &= \sum_l \lambda_l\left(
\tau_{\mathrm{SW}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[p^{sh,(1)}_{d,l}>0\right]
+(\tau_{\mathrm{TP}}-\tau_{\mathrm{SW}})
\mathbf{1}\!\left[p^{sh,(2)}_{d,l}>0\right]\right), \label{eq:cid}\\
\mathrm{EENS}_d &= \sum_l \lambda_l\left(
p^{sh,(1)}_{d,l}\tau_{\mathrm{SW}}
+p^{sh,(2)}_{d,l}(\tau_{\mathrm{TP}}-\tau_{\mathrm{SW}})\right).
\label{eq:eens-nodal}
\end{align}
System EENS is $\sum_d\mathrm{EENS}_d$.  IEEE Std 1366-style system indices are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{SAIFI} = \frac{\sum_d \mathrm{CIF}_d N_d}{\sum_d N_d},
\qquad
\mathrm{SAIDI} = \frac{\sum_d \mathrm{CID}_d N_d}{\sum_d N_d}.
\label{eq:saifi-saidi}
\end{align}
The reported \texttt{saidi\_min} is in minutes per year.

    const std::filesystem::path& case_json,
    const ThreeStageReliabilityOptions& options = {});

    const std::string& case_json_text,
    const ThreeStageReliabilityOptions& options = {});

\end{verbatim}

The historical \texttt{julia\_executable}, \texttt{julia\_project\_dir},
\texttt{cli\_script}, \texttt{workdir}, \texttt{keep\_workdir}, and
\texttt{inherit\_stdio} option fields remain for source compatibility but are
ignored by the native implementation.  The active option is
\texttt{max\_switch\_operations} for Stage~2 tie/switch closing.
